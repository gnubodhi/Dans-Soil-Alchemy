🔥 Step 1: Make Charcoal

Burn your wood/cardboard pile until you’ve got glowing red coals, but stop before it turns to white ash.

Quench flames by limiting oxygen (cover, bury, or smother with soil).

---

💨 Step 2: Steam Activation

While the charcoal is still red-hot, sprinkle water (or throw on damp garden waste).

The steam reacts with the carbon, blasting open pores (C + H₂O → CO + H₂).

You’ll hear hissing and steaming – that’s exactly what you want.

---

🧪 Step 3: Alkali / Ash Coating (KOH source)

While the char is still warm, dust it with wood ash from the same fire (contains KOH, K₂CO₃, CaO, etc.).

The heat helps the alkalis react on the surface, etching more pores and adding potassium as a fertiliser.

Don’t need to drench it — just enough ash to coat the surfaces.

(This is your “poor man’s KOH activation” — not medical-grade, but excellent for soil use.)

---

🕒 Step 4: Let It Sit / Cool

Let the char cool fully with the ash still on it.

At this stage it’s porous, nutrient-coated, but also still “hungry” for nitrogen.

---

🌱 Step 5: Compost Charging

Mix the char+ash into your bokashi or compost bin.

Over a few weeks, microbes will colonise the pores and nutrients (N, P, K) will bind into the carbon matrix.

When you finally put it into soil, it’s already buffered, alive, and ready to support plants.

---

⚠️ Safety tip: do the steaming and ash-sprinkling outdoors and use gloves/mask — ash dust is caustic and steam can scald.
